Before you start

This journal is the training and eating side of The Return. Everything you write here saves as you type, on this device.

The goal is not to look a certain way for a few weeks. It is to be ready for anything life asks of you: carrying a child up a hill, paddling into wind, helping a neighbor move, or getting up off the floor with ease at eighty.

How the 90 days work

  • Three phases of 30 days: Foundation, Build, and Built to Last. Each asks a little more of you.
  • Five short sessions a week, plus 10 minutes of mobility every morning and one long day outside.
  • Simple meals: protein, a carb and vegetables on every plate, cooked at home most of the time.
  • Test yourself on Day 1, Day 45 and Day 90 so you can see the change, not guess at it.

What you need

A floor, a doorway or bar to hang from, a backpack you can load with books or water, and a place to walk. Kettlebells or dumbbells help from Phase 2, but they are not required.

How we train

The whole body, the way our ancestors used it: moving through full ranges, lifting and carrying real loads, and having the engine to keep going.

  1. Movement and mobility, every day.
  2. Functional strength, three days a week: squat, hinge, push, pull, carry and brace.
  3. Conditioning: steady work you can talk through, and hard intervals that teach you to recover.
  4. Recovery: sleep, sunlight, real food and one full rest day. You get stronger while you recover.

How we eat

No counting, no forbidden foods, no shame. Real food, mostly cooked at home, in amounts that leave you satisfied. Enjoy your meals.

The plate

  • Protein first, a palm or two: beef, eggs, fish, chicken, pork, lamb, beans.
  • A carb, a cupped hand or two: rice, potatoes, sweet potato, kalo, fruit, beans. More on hard days.
  • Vegetables, a fist or two: broccoli, greens, carrots, squash, whatever is in season.
  • Real fat for flavor: butter, olive oil, tallow, avocado.

The habits

  1. Three meals a day, not a day of snacks.
  2. Sit down, slow down, phone away. Your body needs about 20 minutes to know it is full.
  3. Cook in batches so good food is the easy choice.
  4. Drink water through the day, more when you sweat.
  5. Eat with your people when you can.

What I actually eat

Ground beef, rice and broccoli. Brown a pound of ground beef with salt, pepper and garlic. Steam broccoli until bright green. Serve over rice with a little butter. Cook a double batch and it is lunch tomorrow.

Eggs, any way. Three or four eggs in butter with rice or potatoes and whatever vegetables are in the fridge.

Meat, starch, green. Steak and sweet potato with greens. Fish with rice and cabbage. Change the pieces, keep the shape.

Slow cooker meals (about 6 servings each)

Shredded beef: 3 lb chuck roast, onion, 4 cloves garlic, 1 cup broth, salt, pepper, cumin. Low 8 hours. Shred and serve with rice, greens and lime.

Kalua-style pork: 3 to 4 lb pork shoulder, 1 tbsp coarse sea salt, a few drops of liquid smoke if you like. Low 10 hours. Serve with rice and steamed cabbage.

Chicken thighs and root vegetables: 2 lb bone-in thighs, 2 lb potatoes, 4 carrots, onion, garlic, thyme, salt, a splash of broth. Low 6 hours.

Beef and bean chili: 2 lb browned ground beef, 2 cans beans, 1 can crushed tomatoes, onion, peppers, chili powder, cumin, salt. Low 6 hours.

Bone broth: beef or chicken bones, onion, carrots, a splash of vinegar, water to cover. Low 24 hours. Sip a mug a day or cook rice in it.
