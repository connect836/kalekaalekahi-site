Wedding Script

A sample ceremony

This is an example of how my typical wedding ceremony unfolds. It includes an optional lei exchange and chant, and every part can be shaped around you, including your own vows, poems and details.

Post-processional introduction

Now that we are all seated and our attention has been brought to this beautiful ceremony, I would like to greet you all with an endearing word we have here in Hawaiʻi, a word that has been a gift to the world. This word is ALOHA! (Group replies with ALOHA.)

We must remind ourselves, everyone, that ALOHA means much more than a simple hello or goodbye. ALO can mean to be in the presence of, or face to face, and HA carries the meaning of exhale, the breath of life. When we enter this world we breathe, and when we leave this world we breathe our last breath, and the wind that we feel is the breath of the world, a gift so we may all breathe. So saying ALOHA to someone is recognizing the life breath that you share. You are telling them that they are a living being, full of life and purpose, that they share in this relationship just as we all do, and that you love them! We are all so very fortunate today because we have two very special people standing before us who love each other very much. What we would like for them to do is gaze into each other's eyes for a moment, say ALOHA, and give a kiss on the cheek.

Presence and centering

Now that we have witnessed (BRIDE) and (GROOM)'s heartfelt greeting to each other, I'd like each of us to say ALOHA to one another, to those sitting next to you and across from you. When we truly see each other, we recognize one another's presence by saying something meaningful.

To begin, I invite us all to truly be here. Take a deep breath in together and exhale. Clear your mind of any thoughts about the past or the future and simply be here now, in this present moment, because this is where the sacred is present. The way we do that, everyone, is to engage all of our senses. Just as these two chose one another to become husband and wife this day, they also chose this place, this island of Maui.

All of you here are the representatives of their relations across the world. That is very significant and lends importance to this day. You are family and friends being brought closer together as these two are being united. So I am asking for your full attention today, everyone, because this celebration of love between (BRIDE) and (GROOM) is just a moment in the expanse of time, and when this moment has passed, what we have left are the memories we create. So let's make some beautiful memories. When we return to our homes and daily lives, we may forget what is most important, and what is most important is happening at this very moment: friends, family, relationship and love! If you all agree with me and want to create beautiful memories, in Hawaiʻi we say ʻAE, which means YES. A loud ʻAE from everyone on three. One, two, three! (Listen for them.)

Optional lei exchange

Let us present to these two the gifts of love we have here for them. Here in Hawaiʻi we have many sayings, and one of them is "E lei nō au i kō aloha," which means I will cherish your love as a beautiful adornment.

In Hawaiʻi, respect, honor and ALOHA, which means compassion, empathy, love and grace, are given to a person when a lei is placed upon their shoulders. This beautifully crafted lei, with its handpicked flowers and twine, is a symbol of never-ending love, but it is also a symbol of things that are fragile and temporal. This lei will hold its beauty for a day or two and then it is gone. Our lives are like these lei in the span of eternity, so live with tender consideration for each other, (BRIDE) and (GROOM). Love one another with all of your hearts, and remember that when you exchange these lei, you are weaving your own lei of love to last for all time. Do you agree to share your gifts of love with each other? (Give the lei to the groom first. When he has placed it, give the lei to the bride.)

Oli Aloha

This gift is a chant and a prayer, whichever word you prefer, because they mean the same thing here in Hawaiʻi. This prayer is called Oli Aloha, a chant of love and greeting. In part, it says that here is the sight that had longed to be seen between these two, a desire that had filled them with eagerness, and now that this moment has arrived, love has been brought with them. That love is evident in all of you here physically, and also in the loved ones, companions, family and friends who have touched their lives in a significant way and may not be here physically but are here in spirit. This prayer recognizes them and acknowledges their presence.

Onaona i ka hala me ka lehua
He hale lehua nō ia na ka noe
O kaʻu nō ia e ʻanoʻi nei
E liʻa nei hoʻi o ka hiki mai
A hiki mai nō ʻoukou
A hiki pū nō me ke aloha
Aloha ē, aloha ē, aloha ē

(As the couple looks into each other's eyes.) There was a seeking of a loved one, (GROOM), and she has been found in (BRIDE). A mate has been found, someone to share the chills of winter and the warmth of summer, a beloved companion to share in the beauty and wonder of this world and in the triumphs over the challenges of life's great journey. Love has made a plea that the two of you be united here on Maui. And everyone, that plea is being answered. The dream is being realized at this very moment, so give them a round of applause to congratulate them on getting married today!

Greeting and welcome

We are all here on this beautiful day of (DATE) to witness the union of (BRIDE) and (GROOM) here at (LOCATION). On this day they have chosen to affirm their love for one another with spoken words, music and prayer, and most importantly with the sharing of sacred vows and the giving of tokens of their everlasting love, their wedding rings.

Every experience you have ever had, everything you have ever done, and every lesson you have learned has brought you here. As you stand before these witnesses today, your family and friends, please look toward them. As you stand here before them, you are taking each other as husband and wife, and we must remind ourselves, everyone, that what we see here are not simply two individuals. They are the totality of all of their experiences and relationships, which includes all of you.

I know it is the hope, prayer and wish of everyone present that the love these two have for each other will grow, strengthen and endure. That they will keep doing the things that made them fall in love in the first place, and do the everyday things for each other that show they are willing to work for this love, this relationship, this marriage.

We all know marriage is love, and marriage also requires each of you to work and to learn with each other. Ask anyone who has been married for many years, and they will tell you that this labor of love will deepen and enrich every part of your lives. You will become better teachers and listeners, better lovers and confidants, better friends and beloveds for each other.

Marriage is love, everyone, and we all know that love is standing before us today, a love that is real and true, because love is what is most important in our lives. It is the creator of our favorite memories and the foundation of our fondest dreams. I have heard it said that the most wonderful thing in life is the discovery of another human being with whom one's relationship grows in depth, beauty and joy as the years increase. This deepening of love between two people is a marvelous thing. It cannot simply be found or wished for; it is called by divine destiny. So it is my own hope, wish and prayer for you that your family and friends, those who love you, will continue to be there for you, to love you, guide you, share with you and support you as you continue on your life's journey to fulfill your destinies together.

If you all agree and promise to be there for them, through the highs and the lows, no matter the circumstance, a loud ʻAE from all of you on three. One, two, three!

Declaration of intent

(GROOM/BRIDE), do you give yourself to (GROOM/BRIDE) this day with complete joy and love, as your partner in this life's journey, and to be a true and faithful (HUSBAND/WIFE)? Do you, with heartfelt intention, commit to be (HIS/HER) companion, to comfort (HIM/HER), respect (HIM/HER), and to cherish, love and honor (HIM/HER) for the rest of your days? If so, say "I do."

Wedding vows (repeated after me)

I, (GROOM/BRIDE), choose you, (GROOM/BRIDE), to be my wedded (HUSBAND/WIFE), to be your companion, equal in love, to be faithful and honest, and to help and support you through loss and success. I shall seek through kindness and understanding to build with you the life we have envisioned. I vow to love you unconditionally, to cherish you entirely, and to care for you sincerely, holding your love as the most precious gift as we make this journey together.

Ring presentation

For as long as time can be remembered, the ring has been a symbol of the completed bond. The ring, as it has been said, is without beginning and without end. Yet we must remind ourselves that there is a generative and creative force that urges life to be created. Just as the two of you stand here in your own circle, beginning a life together, these rings will represent circles of love, circles of life.

These rings are given and received as tokens of enduring and diligent love. With that said, look into each other's eyes.

It is the hope, wish and prayer of all your family and friends, those who care for you most, that the two of you will continue to see the best in each other every day you are together. That you will love one another with all of your hearts, and whenever you are away from each other you will know you are never truly apart. These rings will remind you of the special love you share, the sacred vows you have declared to one another today, and all the memories you hold dear. They will be the physical representation of the spiritual bond and commitment that unites you both. Can you do that for each other?

Exchange of the rings

Now repeat these words after me as you place this ring on your (HUSBAND/WIFE).

This ring is my sacred gift to you, a symbol of my love and a sign that from this day my love will surround you. I give myself to you to be your (HUSBAND/WIFE), and from this moment on, you will never walk alone. I feel so honored to have you as my (HUSBAND/WIFE). I love you with all of my heart. All that I have, and all that I have said, I seal with this ring.

Closing

(GROOM) and (BRIDE), you have been united as one. May you share in each other's joys and console one another in your sorrows. Help each other in all of life, and care for each other truly and deeply, putting this commitment before all else. If you live for your love, your love will continue to live. Trust in life, and most importantly, trust in each other. Being husband and wife is the sweetest and most enduring relationship, so whatever highs and lows you go through, you are now more united than ever before.

My last little prayer for you both: may beauty surround you on the journey ahead, may happiness be your constant companion, and may your days together be good and long upon this Earth. Let us all say MAHALO! Mahalo is much more than a thank you. It is a condition you are in, a state of being, and that is gratitude, honor and appreciation.

May peace and unconditional love surround you both, now and forevermore.

So by the power of all that is sacred, I now pronounce you husband and wife! You may seal your vows with a kiss!

Every ceremony is written with the couple. If you want to change any part of this, we will work through it together.
